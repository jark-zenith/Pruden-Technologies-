# PRUDEN TECHNOLOGIES — Technology Business Blueprint

**Document type:** Founder's master blueprint and requirements register  
**Status:** Planning baseline — not evidence that the business, services, controls, or products are already registered, funded, built, or operational  
**Context:** Kenya-first, globally ambitious; inspired by the systems thinking of fictional Stark Industries, but designed around realistic, lawful, staged execution.

## 1. North star

Build PRUDEN TECHNOLOGIES into a durable technology company that starts with useful digital services and compounds that experience into owned software, AI systems, infrastructure, and eventually advanced research and engineering.

**Operating principle:** BUILD. LEARN. INVENT. EVOLVE.

**Core rule:** Start with a small, trustworthy company that can deliver and earn; do not imitate the capital intensity of a fictional conglomerate before product-market fit, cash flow, qualified staff, and safety systems exist.

## 2. Company architecture and brand governance

Proposed brand architecture; legal structure and final names must be confirmed by the founder and checked for availability before registration or public claims.

- **PRUDEN TECHNOLOGIES** — proposed umbrella commercial brand, business operations, strategy, finance, partnerships, and shared infrastructure.
- **JARK AI TECH INDUSTRIES** — proposed AI, software engineering, developer platforms, agents, automation, and applied R&D division.
- **PRUDEN STUDIOS** — proposed design, creative production, brand, media, and content-services division.
- **Products and ventures** — separate product lines with their own owners, budgets, users, release plans, terms, and success metrics (e.g., Deon, PichaHub, Ivy, JARK AI, and other projects only after each is reviewed).

Requirements:
- [ ] Decide which names are public brands, trading names, registered entities, or internal project names.
- [ ] Check name and trademark availability before investing in identity or filing.
- [ ] Define ownership of code, domains, logos, datasets, models, and customer relationships.
- [ ] Maintain a single source of truth for company description, contact details, pricing, service status, and legal notices.
- [ ] Label all offerings accurately as **idea**, **prototype**, **beta**, **live**, or **retired**.
- [ ] Never market a capability, certification, customer, registration, or deployment that has not been verified.

## 3. Strategic pillars

1. **Revenue engine:** websites, web apps, integrations, design, automation, support, and technical services that customers will pay for now.
2. **Product engineering:** build and maintain a small number of owned products with real users and measurable demand.
3. **AI systems:** model-agnostic assistants, controlled agent workflows, knowledge retrieval, evaluation, and safe automation.
4. **Developer infrastructure:** APIs, identity, observability, billing, documentation, and reusable internal components.
5. **Creative and media technology:** design systems, editing workflows, creator tools, and licensed media products.
6. **Research and advanced engineering:** applied research first; hardware, robotics, IoT, aerospace, energy, and other capital-intensive areas only with expertise, funding, facilities, permissions, and safety review.

## 4. Market and customer requirements

- [ ] Interview 10–20 prospective customers in one target segment before committing to a large build.
- [ ] Start with reachable customers in Kenya: small businesses, professionals, creators, schools, community organizations, and startups.
- [ ] Choose one primary customer segment and one painful problem for the first commercial offer.
- [ ] Document customer problem, current workaround, willingness to pay, alternatives, buying authority, and decision timeline.
- [ ] Validate demand with a landing page, demonstrations, paid pilot, or signed scope—not compliments alone.
- [ ] Define ideal customer profile, competitor map, differentiation, and why the customer should trust PRUDEN.
- [ ] Track leads, conversion, sales cycle, repeat purchases, churn, referrals, and customer satisfaction.
- [ ] Use a written discovery process and avoid promising guaranteed sales, search rankings, virality, or AI accuracy.

## 5. Initial service catalogue

Start only with services the team can competently deliver and support. Expand as capability is demonstrated.

### First-wave offers
- Business websites, landing pages, portfolios, and small web applications.
- Admin dashboards, forms, booking flows, simple customer portals, and integrations.
- Brand identity, flyers, business cards, presentations, and social-media assets through PRUDEN STUDIOS.
- Practical workflow automation, document processing, FAQ assistants, and knowledge-base search with human review where needed.
- Hosting/deployment setup, maintenance, backups, updates, accessibility, and basic security hardening.

### Later offers (readiness-gated)
- Multi-tenant SaaS, API products, voice agents, advanced RAG, data pipelines, mobile/desktop apps, analytics, and larger integrations.
- Cybersecurity assessments only with authorization and suitable competence.
- Health, finance, education, biometric, or other sensitive-data systems only after domain-specific legal, security, and operational review.
- Hardware, robotics, IoT, energy, aerospace, or physical products only with qualified engineering, testing, insurance, supply chain, and regulatory planning.

For every service:
- [ ] Define deliverables, exclusions, timeline, customer responsibilities, revision limits, acceptance criteria, warranty/support period, and change-request pricing.
- [ ] Estimate labor, hosting, model/API use, third-party licences, payment fees, taxes, support, and contingency.
- [ ] Use written quotations, contracts, invoices, milestone payments, and handover notes.
- [ ] Obtain permission for portfolio use, customer logos, screenshots, and testimonials.
- [ ] Define ownership/licensing of deliverables and open-source dependencies.
- [ ] Provide a support route, incident contact, and offboarding/data-export process.

## 7. Finance and business sustainability

- [ ] Create a 12-month operating forecast with conservative, expected, and growth cases.
- [ ] Track cash balance, receivables, payables, project margin, recurring revenue, refunds, tax provisions, and committed spend.
- [ ] Set a monthly budget and approval thresholds for subscriptions, cloud usage, AI tokens, contractors, hardware, and advertising.
- [ ] Maintain an emergency reserve target and do not treat grants, credits, or expected deals as cash until confirmed.
- [ ] Price projects using scope, value, effort, risk, costs, support, and payment fees—not competitor price alone.
- [ ] Define deposits/milestones and stop-work rules for overdue payments.
- [ ] Review every product for gross margin, hosting/model costs, support burden, retention, and customer value.
- [ ] Avoid dependency on a single client, model provider, payment processor, or cloud vendor.
- [ ] Never store payment-card details unless the business has the necessary compliance capability; prefer established payment providers and verify payment callbacks server-side.
- [ ] For M-PESA/Daraja or Paystack, complete provider onboarding, use sandbox tests, verify signatures/callbacks, make payment processing idempotent, reconcile transactions, and never rely on a client-side success screen.

## 8. People, roles, and operating model

Early stage does not require hiring a large team. One person may hold multiple roles, but accountability must be explicit.

Core responsibilities:
- Founder/CEO: strategy, customer discovery, prioritization, partnerships, final approvals.
- Product/engineering lead: architecture, delivery, code quality, technical decisions.
- Design/product experience: user research, workflows, accessibility, design system.
- Finance/operations: budgeting, invoices, contracts, records, vendor renewal.
- Security/privacy owner: access control, risk reviews, incident readiness, data handling.
- Sales/customer success: lead pipeline, onboarding, support, renewals, feedback.
- Independent reviewer/QA: test plans, acceptance checks, release verification.

Requirements:
- [ ] Maintain a skills matrix and identify skill gaps honestly.
- [ ] Use written onboarding, offboarding, confidentiality, IP, acceptable-use, and access-revocation processes.
- [ ] Grant least-privilege access; use individual accounts and MFA.
- [ ] Do not give contractors or AI agents broad access simply for convenience.
- [ ] Use mentors and specialists for legal, accounting, security, and regulated engineering where required.

## 9. Technology architecture

### 9.1 Guiding principles
- Modular, API-first, observable, secure-by-default, mobile-first, accessible, and economical.
- Prefer boring, well-supported technology over unnecessary complexity.
- Start with a modular monolith; split services only when scale, security boundaries, or team ownership justify it.
- Use provider adapters to avoid hard-coupling the business to one model, database, hosting provider, or payment gateway.
- Keep secrets server-side, use environment-specific configuration, and never commit credentials or customer data.
- Design for failure: timeouts, retries with limits, idempotency, queues where needed, graceful degradation, backups, and restore tests.

### 9.2 Recommended initial stack (a starting point, not a mandate)
- Web UI: React + TypeScript + Vite or Next.js; responsive design system and accessibility checks.
- Backend: Node.js + TypeScript (Express/Fastify/Next server functions as appropriate).
- Database: PostgreSQL; migrations, constraints, tenant isolation where relevant.
- Authentication: established auth provider or well-maintained libraries; MFA for privileged users; secure sessions.
- File storage: private object storage with signed URLs, file-size/type limits, malware scanning when appropriate, and retention controls.
- Hosting: select a provider based on cost, region, reliability, data location, backups, and operational skill; keep staging separate from production.
- CI/CD: GitHub, pull requests, automated tests, dependency/security checks, preview environments, controlled production releases.
- Observability: structured logs, metrics, error tracking, uptime checks, request IDs, and redaction of secrets/personal data.
- Email/notifications: established providers; delivery status, unsubscribe/consent rules, and abuse prevention.
- Payments: provider-hosted checkout/SDK and verified server-side webhooks; separate sandbox and live credentials.
- AI: provider abstraction, model allowlist, usage budgets, prompt/version tracking, evaluation datasets, safety filters, and fallback behavior.

Do not add Kubernetes, microservices, a custom model-training cluster, or expensive hardware until there is a measured requirement and budget.

### 9.3 Shared platform capabilities
- [ ] Identity, accounts, roles, organizations/tenants, and permission checks.
- [ ] Central configuration and secret management.
- [ ] Shared audit event format with actor, action, target, timestamp, outcome, and correlation ID.
- [ ] Notifications, job processing, retry policy, and dead-letter handling where appropriate.
- [ ] API versioning, schema validation, rate limits, abuse controls, and documented errors.
- [ ] Data export/deletion and retention jobs.
- [ ] Backups, restoration drills, migration rollback plan, and incident playbooks.
- [ ] Feature flags and staged rollout.
- [ ] Service catalogue, dependency register, ownership, health checks, and support contacts.
- [ ] Cost attribution per project/customer/model and alert thresholds.

## 10. AI and agent workforce architecture

Treat AI assistants as supervised software components with bounded permissions—not as employees with unrestricted authority.

Suggested role boundaries:
- **PRUDEN Command:** founder-facing operating console; goals, tasks, projects, decisions, metrics, approvals, and portfolio overview.
- **Research/strategy agent:** gathers evidence and drafts options; cites sources; cannot commit funds or make external promises.
- **Product planner:** turns approved goals into requirements, milestones, and acceptance tests.
- **Engineering executor:** works in an isolated repository/branch or sandbox; no production secrets by default.
- **QA/security reviewer:** independently tests changes, checks access boundaries, dependencies, and failure modes.
- **Operations assistant:** drafts reports, invoices, and messages; human approval before sending or changing records.
- **Customer support assistant:** uses approved knowledge and escalates uncertainty, complaints, privacy requests, and high-risk issues.

Required controls:
- [ ] Define each agent's owner, purpose, allowed tools, data scope, budget, time limit, and prohibited actions.
- [ ] Use a task queue and shared state rather than relying on separate chat histories.
- [ ] Use one designated writer per task/repository at a time; branch, commit, test, and open a PR for review.
- [ ] Separate planning, execution, and independent verification.
- [ ] Require explicit human approval for production deployment, destructive database operations, account/access changes, spending, payments/refunds, external commitments, customer campaigns, and legal/regulated decisions.
- [ ] Require confirmation for actions that cannot be easily reversed.
- [ ] Log tool calls, approvals, failures, model/version, cost, and affected resources without logging secrets.
- [ ] Enforce tool allowlists, scoped credentials, sandboxing, rate limits, budgets, kill switch, and rollback.
- [ ] Defend against prompt injection in documents/web pages, data exfiltration, unsafe code execution, insecure output handling, and confused-deputy behavior.
- [ ] Test agent behavior with adversarial scenarios, regression suites, and human evaluation before increasing permissions.
- [ ] Never give an agent unrestricted shell, personal account credentials, production database access, or authority to publish/deploy merely because it can do so.
- [ ] Label generated outputs and preserve human accountability for consequential decisions.

## 11. Security baseline

Use a recognized risk-management approach such as NIST CSF 2.0, alongside secure software development practices and current OWASP guidance.

Minimum controls before handling real customer data:
- [ ] MFA on GitHub, email, cloud, domain registrar, database, and payment accounts.
- [ ] Password manager, unique credentials, recovery codes stored securely.
- [ ] Least privilege and periodic access review.
- [ ] Separate development, staging, and production environments.
- [ ] Secret scanning and immediate rotation if a secret is exposed.
- [ ] Input validation, output encoding, parameterized queries, CSRF protections where applicable, secure cookies, CORS allowlists, and SSRF protections.
- [ ] Authentication and authorization tested server-side for every protected action and object.
- [ ] Rate limiting, abuse detection, bot protection where warranted, and upload restrictions.
- [ ] Encryption in transit and at rest through supported services; managed keys where appropriate.
- [ ] Dependency pinning/updates, vulnerability alerts, software bill of materials when appropriate, and licence review.
- [ ] Audit logs for privileged actions; restrict access and set retention.
- [ ] Secure error messages and redacted logs; no tokens, passwords, private prompts, or sensitive records in telemetry.
- [ ] Backups and tested restoration; documented incident response and notification obligations.
- [ ] Threat model for each product that stores personal, financial, health, or confidential business data.
- [ ] Penetration testing or specialist review proportionate to risk before high-impact launches.

## 12. Product development and release lifecycle

Every product must pass through explicit gates:

1. **Discover:** problem statement, customer evidence, alternatives, user and business value.
2. **Define:** requirements, non-goals, data classification, risks, acceptance criteria, cost ceiling.
3. **Design:** architecture, user flows, data model, permissions, threat model, failure handling.
4. **Build:** small issues, branches, code review, migrations, automated tests, documentation.
5. **Verify:** functional, accessibility, security, performance, responsive, backup/restore, and cost tests as appropriate.
6. **Pilot:** limited audience, clear feedback route, rollback, support owner, incident watch.
7. **Release:** approval, changelog, monitoring, rollback plan, customer communication.
8. **Operate:** support, incident handling, patches, usage/cost monitoring, backups.
9. **Review or retire:** measure value; migrate/export user data; revoke credentials; archive records according to policy.

Release acceptance checklist:
- [ ] Required user journeys pass on mobile and desktop.
- [ ] Authorization is verified on the server, not just hidden in the UI.
- [ ] No critical/high unresolved security issue without documented risk acceptance.
- [ ] Tests and build pass; migrations are safe and recoverable.
- [ ] Privacy notice, terms, support contact, and pricing are accurate.
- [ ] Monitoring, backups, and rollback are ready.
- [ ] Cost/usage limits are configured.
- [ ] A named human accepts release responsibility.

## 13. Data and information governance

- [ ] Maintain an inventory of data collected, source, purpose, legal basis/authority, location, access, recipients, and retention.
- [ ] Classify data: public, internal, confidential, sensitive/restricted.
- [ ] Collect the minimum data needed; avoid collecting data “just in case”.
- [ ] Define retention and deletion, including backups and third-party processors.
- [ ] Provide a route for access, correction, deletion, objection, and complaints where applicable.
- [ ] Review processors, subprocessors, model providers, hosting regions, and cross-border transfers.
- [ ] Do not use customer data to train or improve models unless permitted, transparent, and appropriately authorized.
- [ ] Obtain consent/permission for recording, voice, images, testimonials, and marketing communications where required.
- [ ] Keep development data synthetic or properly anonymized.
- [ ] Create separate access rules for health, financial, children’s, biometric, and other sensitive data.

## 16. Infrastructure, workspace, and equipment

### Start now
- Reliable laptop/Chromebook workflow, phone, internet/data backup, secure account recovery, cloud-based version control, and lightweight development environments.
- Shared password manager, domain/email, project tracker, documentation, accounting/recordkeeping, and customer support channel.
- Cloud hosting and managed services with clear quotas and alerts.

### Add only when justified
- Dedicated development workstation, test phones, backup internet/power, NAS or managed backup, test devices, lab equipment, dedicated office/studio, and paid monitoring.
- For physical engineering: qualified supervision, risk assessments, lab procedures, PPE, equipment maintenance, calibration, insurance, and applicable permits.

Do not buy servers, a GPU rig, office space, or lab equipment solely to look like a large company. Buy based on customer demand, technical need, and cash-flow capacity.

## 17. Metrics and management dashboard

The company operating dashboard should report:
- **Finance:** cash runway, monthly revenue, recurring revenue, margin, receivables, expenses, cloud/API spend.
- **Sales:** leads, qualified pipeline, proposal conversion, sales cycle, average deal size, repeat customers.
- **Delivery:** active projects, on-time completion, defects, rework, support backlog, customer satisfaction.
- **Products:** active users, activation, retention, churn, usage, conversion, uptime, cost per active user.
- **Engineering:** deployment frequency, change failure rate, recovery time, test health, open vulnerabilities, dependency freshness.
- **Security/privacy:** access review status, incidents, overdue patches, backup restore test, privacy requests, vendor reviews.
- **AI operations:** cost per task, latency, tool-call failures, evaluation scores, human override/escalation rate, safety incidents.
- **People:** capacity, workload, skills gaps, contractor spend, onboarding/offboarding status.

Every metric must have a definition, source, owner, review cadence, and a decision it informs. Avoid vanity metrics.

## 18. Risk register

Maintain a living risk register with likelihood, impact, owner, mitigation, trigger, and review date. Initial risks:
- Founder bandwidth and burnout.
- Low or inconsistent customer demand.
- Underpricing and scope creep.
- Unpaid invoices and cash-flow shortages.
- Vendor lock-in or provider outages.
- API/model costs exceeding revenue.
- Security breach, lost device, credential theft, or accidental data exposure.
- AI hallucinations, prompt injection, unsafe tool execution, or unauthorized external actions.
- IP/licensing disputes and unclear client ownership.
- Legal/tax/privacy obligations misunderstood.
- Unverified claims or unreliable production releases.
- Hardware, streaming, payment, or regulated product risks.
- Loss of project context across multiple AI tools and collaborators.

## 21. Definition of “Stark-inspired” maturity

PRUDEN should earn its way through these capability levels:

1. **Level 1 — Reliable studio:** professional service delivery, documented operations, repeat customers, controlled costs.
2. **Level 2 — Product company:** one or more useful products, recurring value, support, metrics, and predictable releases.
3. **Level 3 — AI-enabled operations:** bounded agents, reusable tools, traceable decisions, approvals, independent evaluation.
4. **Level 4 — Platform company:** stable APIs, identity, billing, integrations, multi-tenant controls, partner ecosystem.
5. **Level 5 — Applied R&D lab:** funded experiments, qualified collaborators, reproducible research, IP strategy, safety reviews.
6. **Level 6 — Advanced engineering group:** specialized hardware/robotics/energy/aerospace or other physical systems only after financing, expertise, facilities, compliance, and validation.

Progress is based on evidence and readiness gates—not dates or aesthetics.
